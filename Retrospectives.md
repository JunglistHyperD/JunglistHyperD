
# Running Retrospectives

A Team could be modelled as a Dynamic System which is in a constant state of change. However, unlike machines, people are not fungible objects to be swapped in or out, but persons with personalities, perspectives and different drivers for what is important to them. A retrospective helps level set all those differences into a common understanding which can bring the team closer together with each other, and drive the team forward on its mission.  This is a guide on one way to run a retrospective. Choose your Vehicle for the wall chart: Hot Airballoon, Sailboat, Race car etc.  A Retrospective *should* be done at a regular healthy cadence for the team, as well as **every time a new person joins the team.** A Retrospective should not be rushed and depending on the Team, the situation and facilitator; can take a few hours. The Timings below are a minimum.

1. **Ice Breaker - 2 mins** 
	- This helps bring everyone from where they are coming from ( another meeting, a deep project etc ) and into the room/session.

2. **Open (5 min).** Explain why you're there: in order to reflect as a team and grow as a team, and state clearly that it is NOT about assigning blame to any one individual. Its about collective growth. 

3. **Timeline (5-10 min).** Walk through and agree on a rough timeline of what has happened since the last time or in the window under review, using facts as the markers like: a project, a trip, a delivery, some system incident including "These alerts, then these logs and after we saw messages". Agree on one shared account of events, and don't let perfection get in the way of good.

4. **Silent Ideation (10–15 min).** On a Whiteboard (Digital is ok but it's best in person) Everyone zooms into their own corner of the sailboat/hot air balloon page and writes post-it notes in silence, one idea per note answering either "what lifted us up?", "what pulled us down?" .
  For inspiration....
  - what helped, 
  - what slowed us down, 
	- communication, 
	- surprises, 
	- luck, 
	- one change,
	- risks ahead.
    
	It is very important to do this away from others to not distract them, or influence their own expression of what they have noticed. It is also important that your written idea is evergreen and can be understood the week later, if we need to break the session.

6. **Converge, Clarify and Sort (10 min).** Each person places their notes above or below the Balloon. As a team we automagically (it is magic) group similar notes into themes as we go and merge duplicates. At this stage questions are only for clarification on the post-it.

7. **Distillation in to themes (5 min).** Rewrite each cluster as a short statement:
	1. Things that lifted us up, written as “Keep: …”
	2. Things that pulled us down, written as problems to solve “How might we…?”

8. **Two-colour dot vote (5–10 min).** 
	Dot voting is a quick way to set priorities and allows every voice to be counted equally instead of the loudest one winning. Each person votes a limited number of sticky dots across the options they think matter most. They can even vote twice or three times on the same thing. The whole group’s priorities become visible at a glance.
	A common rule of thumb is to give each person about **a third as many dots as there are options after distillation**. 
	Eg; With around 6 themes on a side, that works out to:
	- **2 dots per colour, per side**
	- That’s **8 dots per person** in total (2 colours × 2 sides × 2 dots)
	Everyone gets a few dots of each colour for each end of the balloon. 
	- **Green dots, “matters most”:** the most valuable to keep (lift side), or the most damaging if it happens again (pull side).
	- **Blue dots, “needs attention now”:** the most fragile, or at risk of being lost (lift side), or the most likely to happen again (pull side).

9. **Sanity check (5 min).** Check that nothing high-risk was missed because it got few votes. If something was, add it to the top themes.

10. **Confirm what we keep (5 min).** Turn the top 2 or 3 lift-side themes into working agreements, written as “We…”, for example: _We give the customer clear, regular updates during an incident._ Read them out and check everyone agrees.

11. **Triage the problems (10–15 min).** Take the top 2 or 3 pull-down themes, one at a time, with about 3 to 5 minutes on each. Ask: _“Do we know what the fix is?”_
    - **Small (yes, and it’s clear):** create an action.
    - **Medium (yes, but it’s big):** agree only the first step as the action.
    - **Large (no, the cause or solution is unclear):** the action is to investigate. One owner looks into it and brings options to a short follow-up meeting. Every action eventually needs an owner, a date and a “done when”. If one person can’t finish it within about two weeks, break it down further.

12. **Close (5 min).**
    - Read back the working agreements and actions.
    - Add any themes you didn’t get to to a "car park".
    - Agree who writes up the summary, who shares it, and by when.
    - Set the date for any follow-up meetings.

13. **After the session.**
    - Add the working agreements to the team’s charter or working agreement.
    - Track the actions somewhere visible, like the team board or backlog.
    - Start the next retro by reviewing last time’s actions: done, in progress, or dropped, and why.
